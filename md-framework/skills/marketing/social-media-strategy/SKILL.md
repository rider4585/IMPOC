## Social Media Strategy

For a local shop, social media is primarily a **local awareness and reminder**
channel, not a sales channel. Judged as a sales channel it almost always looks
like a failure; judged as cheap local presence it is often worthwhile.

Be honest about which you are doing, and set expectations accordingly.

### Decide whether it is worth the time

Social media is not free — it costs the scarcest resource a small shop has, which
is attention. Before committing:

- Are your customers actually on the platform you are considering?
- Is there someone who will post consistently, week after week?
- What would you do with the time instead? Better in-store execution often
  returns more
- Can you measure anything, even roughly?

**Consistent presence on one platform beats scattered presence on four.** An
abandoned profile is worse than none — it signals a shop that may also have
closed.

### Choose by audience, not by trend

| Platform | Good for a shop | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **WhatsApp Status / Business profile** | Existing customers, high visibility, zero cost | Time only |
| **Instagram** | Visual products, local discovery, younger customers | Time; photos matter |
| **Facebook** | Local community groups, older demographics, events | Time |
| **Google Business Profile** | **Highest value for most shops** — appears in local search and maps | Low, mostly one-off |

**Google Business Profile first.** Hours, address, photos, and reviews affect
whether people find and choose the shop at the moment they are looking for it.
That is a higher-intent audience than any social feed, and it is largely a
one-time setup with occasional updates.

### Content that works for a shop

The mistake is treating it as advertising. What performs is evidence that the
shop is real, active, and worth visiting.

| Works | Does not |
|---|---|
| New stock arrivals | Generic promotional graphics |
| "Back in stock" for things people ask for | Reposted stock imagery |
| Behind the counter, staff, the shop itself | Motivational quotes |
| Local relevance — events, weather, festivals | Anything not about this shop |
| Practical information — hours, closures | Frequent hard-sell posts |
| Answering questions publicly | Ignoring comments |

Use the data you already have: `recurring-requests` tells you what people asked
for, and `product-velocity` tells you what is newly popular. "The rice you keep
asking for is back" is a better post than any designed graphic.

### Cadence: sustainable beats frequent

**Two or three posts a week, sustained**, beats daily posting for a fortnight
followed by silence. Set a cadence someone can actually maintain during a busy
week.

Batch the work: photograph several items at once, write posts in one sitting, and
schedule them. Ad hoc posting does not survive a busy trading period.

### Respond, always

Responsiveness matters more than posting volume.

- Answer questions quickly — an unanswered "do you have X?" is a lost sale
- Respond to reviews, especially negative ones, briefly and without defensiveness
- Never argue publicly; move a dispute to a private channel
- A complaint answered well in public is visible evidence of good service

Route substantive complaints into `complaint-analysis` rather than treating them
as social media issues.

### Measure honestly, and expect little

Attribution is genuinely hard here. Do not manufacture precision.

| Measure | Reliability |
|---|---|
| "Did you see us online?" asked at the till | Rough but real |
| Google Business Profile insights (searches, direction requests, calls) | **Good** — high intent |
| A code or offer mentioned only in a post | Good, if used |
| Follower count | Weak — a vanity metric |
| Likes and reach | Weak |

**Direction requests and calls from Google Business Profile are the most
meaningful numbers available** to a local shop — they represent someone deciding
to visit.

Accept that most social media effect is unattributable. That is a reason to keep
the cost low, not a reason to fabricate a number.

### Set the expectation with the owner

Say plainly: for a local shop, social media is unlikely to drive measurable
sales directly. Its value is being findable, looking active, and staying in mind.
If the owner expects it to produce a measurable revenue increase, that
expectation should be corrected before the effort is invested — not afterwards.

Where budget is limited, **in-store execution and Google Business Profile almost
always return more** than paid social.

### Practical rules

- Never post customer information or images of customers without permission
- Never post anything identifying a staff member without their agreement
- Keep stated hours and information accurate — an out-of-date profile costs
  visits
- Do not promote products you cannot supply — check stock first
- Do not buy followers; it damages reach and signals nothing

### Checklist

- [ ] Decided whether it is worth the time, against alternatives
- [ ] Google Business Profile complete and accurate — done first
- [ ] One or two platforms chosen by where customers actually are
- [ ] Sustainable cadence agreed and batched
- [ ] Content drawn from real shop activity and request data
- [ ] Stock verified before promoting anything
- [ ] Questions and reviews answered promptly
- [ ] Complaints routed to proper analysis
- [ ] Measurement limited to what is genuinely attributable
- [ ] Vanity metrics not reported as results
- [ ] Owner's expectations set honestly
- [ ] Permission obtained before posting people
